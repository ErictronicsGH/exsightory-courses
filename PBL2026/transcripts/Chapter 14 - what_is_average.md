- What is Average?
    There are several different kinds of "average" and all of them are valid int eright situations. The word average refers to the most typical value, or most expected value, in a collection of numerical data.

    When you collect data, there are a number of ways that the results can vary from one observation to another even when conditions are supposed to be the same.

    First is there is old-fashinoned measurment and observational error. Not all rullers are truly the same ad not all applications of the same ruler to the same object turn out the same. Inshort there will always be some variability in the measurements from one trail to another.

    - There can be some actual variability in the events being recorded. One way tot handle these variations is to find the most typical value for the lot. 

    - THere are actually several differnt kinds of averages but the most commonly encountered are the arithmetic mean (usually called simplmy the mean), the median, and the mode. These are each a little diferent from the others and all of them are correct "averages" when used in the right context. 

    - The arithmetic mean is the common type of average. It is the sum of all the values divided by the number of values.

    - The median is another type of average: it is the middle value in the data set, that is the value where exactly half of the values are above it and half are below it. 

    - The mode is also an average of sorts and it defined as the most frequently occurring value in a data set. 

    - Different averages are used for different situations. If the data are perfectly symmetrical, then the mean, median, and mode are the same number. If the mean, median and mode are not the same, then the data are not symmertrical around the mean and the difference is a test of that symmetry.

    - When the mean, median and mode are symmetric or at least almost symmetric then the mean could be used.

    - There are situations where the mean is not terribly useful especially if one or two data ponts have very large or very small values compared with the rest of the data.

    - The mean is best used when the data are symmetrical;
    -  The median is often used when the data are highly asymmetrical due to outliers; 
    - The mode is usually used to answer the question of what is the most occurring event.

    - Other averages include geometric mean, and the harmonic mean. 
    - Geometric mean is often used when the data are not very symmetrical, especially in biological studies. Ssuming you have 48 cedis to spend and you have spend one-half of your available money each day for 5 days. The data would tabulate as follows

    Day     Amount
    1       48
    2       24
    3       12
    4       6
    5       3

    The arithmetic mean in this case will be 18.6 cedis. However if we graph these values the line connecting the top of the bar graphs is not straight but rather traces and exponential curve which will require a logarithmic treatment to get the median. which will be 11.99

    - Harmonic mean is a bit more complicated and is used when data is expressed as ratios. HM is the reciprocal of the mean of the reciprocals of the data


    - Integrated, Root Mean Square (RMS), and Root Sum Squares (RSS) Averages
        These are averages sometimes used in science, engineering and technology.

        - Integrated Average is the area under the curve of the function divided by the segment of the range overwhich the avaerage is taken.
        The integrated average is ofeten found in electronics circuits where either an RC low-pass filter or an RC operational amplifier circuit called Miller Integrator is used, and where the filter has an RC product much greater than the period of the applied input waveform. The output of the circuit is proportional to the time average of the input signal.

        - The root mean square (rms) value is used extensively in electrical circuits and certain other technologies. For example, a sine wave altenating current (ac) wave may be compared with the direct (DC) voltage level what will produce the same amount of heating in an electrical resistance. The value of the ac wave that is the dc heating equivalent is the root mean square (rms) value. 

        - The Root Sum Square (RSS) is used in cases where different data are combined to form a single number, even though the data are in no way correlated with one another. FOr example, noise signals in electronics circuits are errors and come from several different sources. Supposed we have n independent noise voltage sources where these sources are truly independent of teach other, they cannot be simply combined in a linear additve manner but rather must be combined using the RSS method. The RSS number is sometimes used to define a single-valued error term from a number of uncorrelated error terms or alternatively, to find a  single standard error or a number of measurements of the same value.
