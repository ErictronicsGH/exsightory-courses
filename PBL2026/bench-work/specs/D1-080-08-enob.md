## 8. Faithfulness (the quality bar)

**Arithmetic** (ENOB = (SINAD − 1.76)/6.02; recomputed in Node, 2026-10-20):
- **The constants**: 20·log₁₀ 2 = **6.0206 dB** (6.02 ✓; D1·060's ×2 → +6.021 dB, S15 ✓); 10·log₁₀ 1.5 = **1.7609 dB**
  (1.76 ✓). Derivation: a full-scale sine of 2^N steps has RMS 2^N·q/(2√2); uniform quantization error has RMS q/√12;
  their ratio is 2^N·√12/(2√2) = 2^N·√1.5; 20·log₁₀(2^N·√1.5) = 6.0206·N + 1.7609 dB ✓.
- **E1**: 6.02 × 12 + 1.76 = 72.24 + 1.76 = **74.00 dB** (the PLAN's and the Flow's 74.0 ✓; with exact constants

## 14. Findings for the owner

- **F1 — accuracy: the explain says the 1.76 dB is what "a perfect quantizer already loses".** The PLAN's explain:
  *"62 dB less the 1.76 dB a perfect quantizer already loses leaves 60.2 dB"*. A perfect N-bit converter's SNR is 6.02·N
  **+** 1.76 dB: the 1.76 dB is what a full-scale sine scores *over* the quantization noise beyond 6.02 dB a bit
  (10·log₁₀ 1.5, from the sine's RMS against the noise's q/√12), not a loss. The arithmetic is right; the reason given
  for it is backwards, and the learners read it on the reveal. Options: (a) *"Each bit is worth 6.02 dB; take off the
  1.76 dB that a full-scale sine scores on top of that even on a perfect converter, and 62 dB leaves 60.2 dB, which is ten
  bits' worth."* (**recommended**); (b) *"…62 dB less the formula's 1.76 dB (a full-scale sine's share) leaves 60.2 dB…"*;
  (c) *"…62 dB less 1.76 dB leaves 60.2 dB…"* (no reason given). +0 min (the explain is read, not spoken).
- **F2 — Proposal: the Proof from p1, before the numeric.** The PLAN's reveal is *"the Equation below"*, so the bench
  waits for p5, and p1–p2 (two formulas, 70 words) are spoken with nothing on the bench. The Flow states the formula before
  the question, so showing the derivation (steps 3–5) at p1–p2 gives nothing away; the Graph and Example panes, which do
