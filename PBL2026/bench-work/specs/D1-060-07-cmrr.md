## 1. What the room must understand

- **Statement** (verbatim): *No amplifier ignores the common-mode voltage completely. Its common-mode rejection ratio,
  in dB, says how much of that large voltage leaks into the small signal.*
- **SHOW** (verbatim from the PLAN): *Equation — input-referred error = V_cm / 10^(CMRR/20), graphed against CMRR from
  40 to 120 dB at V_cm = 12 V, beside the 20 mV signal.*
- **Look for** (verbatim): *1.2 mV (6 %) at 80 dB, 0.12 mV (0.6 %) at 100 dB.*
- **Status** (verbatim): *ready (ideal: the in-amp part's common-mode reveal).*
- **Bench and library.** The **bench** is the Equation Bench. The **library** is this card's equation, `cmrr-error`.
  The PLAN's ideal, the in-amp part on the Electronics Bench, waits for that bench (in development).
- **Insight**: *the common-mode voltage leaks in, divided by the CMRR as a ratio; at the high side that turns the whole
  supply into error, and at the low side there is almost nothing to leak.*
- **Kind of knowledge**: *a relation between quantities* (the error against CMRR) and *a contrast between two states*
  (high side against low side). Card test → **Equation**. B17 applies (the two sides on one axis, beside the signal's
  level). B15, B18 and B19 do not.
- **Why this bench, not a still**: the ramp from 80 to 100 dB shows the error falling tenfold for 20 dB. The two curves
  show the same amplifier making a 6 % error at one end of the shunt and a 0.005 % error at the other. The boundary
  shows how much CMRR the high side needs before the error is under 1 %.
- **Key variable**: CMRR, at a given V_cm.

## 8. Faithfulness (the quality bar)

**Arithmetic** (V_e = V_cm / 10^(CMRR/20)):
- E1: 10^(80/20) = 10⁴; 12 / 10⁴ = **1.200 mV**; 1.2 / 20 = **6.0 %** ✓ (the PLAN's Look for).
- E2: 10^5; 12 / 10⁵ = **0.1200 mV**; **0.60 %** ✓.
- E3: the low side's inputs sit at 20 mV and 0 V, so V_cm = (20 + 0)/2 = **10 mV**; 0.010 / 10⁴ = **1.000 µV**;
  **0.005 %**.
- E4 (the PLAN's FAULT): a unity-gain difference amplifier's worst-case CMRR from resistors of tolerance t is ≈ (1 +
  G)/(4t) = 2 / 0.04 = **50 = 33.98 dB** (*around 34 dB* ✓); 12 / 50 = **0.240 V** ✓; 0.240 / 0.020 = **12** ✓
  (*twelve times the full-scale shunt signal*). At exactly 34 dB, 12 / 10^1.7 = 0.2394 V.
- The boundaries cross the high side at 20·log(12/0.02) = 20·log 600 = **55.6 dB** (error equal to the signal) and
  20·log(12/0.0002) = 20·log 60,000 = **95.6 dB** (under 1 %).
- The graph's ends: 12 V at 40 dB is 0.120 V; at 120 dB, 12.0 µV. 10 mV at 40 dB is 100 µV; at 120 dB, **10 nV**,
  the axis's floor.
- **Card 6's output, made real** (A7): at G = 100, 1.2 mV referred to the input is 0.12 V at the output, so the high
  side reads 2.0 ± 0.12 V where card 6 showed 2.0 V. The sign depends on the part's mismatch. Card 6's demonstration
  shows the ideal, and card 7's reveal shows the shift (for card 6's demonstration brief).
- **Real values** (cited here only): instrumentation amplifiers of the classic three-op-amp class quote DC CMRR of about
  80 dB at G = 1 rising to 100–120 dB at G = 100 and above (e.g. INA128-class: about 120 dB at G = 100, 113–120 dB minimum by grade). The PLAN's 80
  and 100 dB are therefore conservative for an in-amp at G = 100, and realistic for cheaper parts or at a few kHz (F4).

## 14. Findings for the owner

- **F1 — Card 6's 2.0 V at both sides.** Card 6's demonstration shows the ideal in-amp (2.0 V at the high and low side).
  Card 7 reveals that a real one at 80 dB reads 2.0 ± 0.12 V at the high side. This is consistent, because the Flow
  calls card 6's amplifier ideal. Noted for card 6's demonstration brief: the same scene, a CMRR switch, and the reading
  moving by 0.12 V.
- **F2 — "Beside the 20 mV signal" as two boundaries.** The Equation's graph draws curves and shaded regions, not
  reference lines. The signal's level is drawn as the boundary *V_e < 20 mV* (*smaller than the signal*), with a second
  boundary *V_e < 0.2 mV* (*under 1 % of it*), which shows that the high side needs about 96 dB. Keep both
  (**recommended**), or the first only?
- **F3 — Proposal: the low side as a second curve** (`compare` V_cm at 12 V and 10 mV). p6 says *"At the low side the
  common-mode voltage is almost zero, and CMRR hardly matters"*; the curve shows it 1,200 times lower. The y axis
  reaches down to 10 nV so that the whole curve shows. Take (**recommended**), or the high side only, as the PLAN?
- **F4 (scientific accuracy) — 80 dB is low for an in-amp at G = 100.** Three-op-amp in-amps of the class give 100–120
  dB at G = 100 and DC. 80 dB is typical at G = 1, or at G = 100 a few kHz up. The PLAN's numbers still teach the point.
  The options:
  - (a) keep 80 and 100 dB, and add the assumption *at the gain and frequency in use* (**recommended**: the Flow's p6
    already warns about both);
  - (b) use 100 and 120 dB (12 µV, 0.06 %), which weakens the lesson.
- **F5 — Proposals**: the teacher tip (§7d) and the suggested words for s4 (+0.1 min).
