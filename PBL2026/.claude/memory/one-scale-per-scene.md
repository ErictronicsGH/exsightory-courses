Owner, 2026-10-10, mid-build of D1·030 card 1: *"remember the 6 gates. realism is important"*. The first draft had the
desk lamp at ≈ 0.6 px/mm beside boards at ≈ 3 px/mm (boards four times too big): it looked fine and failed gate 1's
"proportions to one stated scale".

**Why:** mixed scales on one bench make the scene a diagram of glyphs; the owner treats realism as a first-class gate.

**How to apply:** before drawing a scene, pick one scale (state it in the README, and on the stage when it matters) and size
every part from its real dimensions (header pitch 2.54 mm, Dupont shell 2.5 × 14 mm, 5 mm LDR…). If that makes a large
object too big, bring the camera close and let it leave the frame (card 1: only the lamp's head and arm); if it makes small
print unreadable, add a magnifier (D.zoomBubble), as card 9's enlarged board. Check proportions before sending, not after.

**Devices of very different sizes (D1·030 card 1, 4e, 2026-10-10):** the owner asked for a phone, the board and a laptop
"rendered proportionally according to their physical size", bases aligned, *and* the board emphasised as "the main thing".
Panels marked "not to scale" were rejected; pulling the board forward in depth did not make it lead. Chosen (asked, owner
picked it): one true-scale 3D scene through one camera, plus a ×2 lens on the main object from a detail render of the same
view. Then, seeing it, the owner overrode: "Dont do the lens thing just make the Arduino bigger" and gave a reference picture
(phone | board | laptop, front view, bases on one line, the phone ≈ 1.3 × the board's height): the board drawn 150 mm tall, the
phone 1.3 ×, the laptop to scale, the departure **said on the stage** (gate 2). Lesson: when proportion and emphasis conflict,
ask for (or offer to follow) the owner's reference arrangement first; state any enlargement on the stage.
